Import from OsmAnd

In OsmAnd, open My Places, then share a track or your favourites as a GPX file and save it to Downloads. Then choose it here. Files are read on this phone and never uploaded.

Everything in one OsmAnd export: in OsmAnd's export (.osf), tick your tracks, favourites, regional map and contour lines, save it, and choose it here. The app imports your points and tracks first, then pulls creeks, ponds, wetlands and contours around them. This all happens on the phone; keep that file to yourself, since it holds your locations.

Map only: if you import your points another way first, an export with just your regional map works too. The app pulls water features within 1.5 km of your plan, on this phone. If you export contour lines too, they become your elevation.

Sharp imagery from OsmAnd: if you use an imagery map in OsmAnd, download its tiles for the area around your property (zoom 16–18 is plenty), then include that map in your OsmAnd export. The app stitches the tiles around your plan, on this phone. Keep the area small, and check the imagery provider's terms.

Sentinel-2 satellite image: in the Copernicus Browser (free account), zoom out so your property is well inside the view and off-centre, pick a recent clear date, choose the True colour layer, then Download > Analytical: image format TIFF (8-bit), coordinate system WGS 84, highest resolution. Choose the file here. The app keeps only the part around your plan, on this phone.

Tracing a creek yourself: in OsmAnd, open Menu > Plan a route, switch the profile to Straight line, and tap along the creek (or the bottom of the ravine) on the map. Save it as a track with "creek" or "ravine" in the name, then share it here. Tracing from upstream to downstream sets the flow direction; otherwise heights decide it, or you can reverse it under Lines.

Elevation: tracks you record with OsmAnd already store a height at every point. The more of the property you walk with recording on, especially ridges and low ground, the better the downhill arrows get. Phone GPS heights are rough (often off by several metres), so treat small slopes with caution.

Suggestions follow rules of thumb: sit downwind or crosswind of deer travel, never between deer and their bedding, within shooting range, and walk in from the downwind side. They don't know your terrain or cover. Check legal hours and rules for your zone before you hunt.
Privacy: this app has no server and is blocked from making network requests. Points, tracks and sightings live in this phone's browser storage until you export or share them. Clearing Chrome's site data erases them, so save a backup now and then.
